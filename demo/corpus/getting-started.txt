Getting Started

A workspace is the unit of everything: documents, conversations, billing, and access all belong to one workspace. Most customers run a single workspace; some keep a separate one per client or per department when the documents must not be searchable together.

To add documents, open Documents and upload. Plain text, Markdown, and PDF are supported. A single file may be up to 25 MB. There is no limit on the number of documents beyond your plan's storage allowance.

After upload, a document is split into passages of roughly 1,200 characters with a 200-character overlap between neighbours. The overlap matters: a sentence that straddles a boundary would otherwise be split across two passages and match neither well. Each passage is then converted into a numerical representation that captures its meaning, which is what makes it findable by a question phrased differently from the text.

Indexing usually takes a few seconds for a short document and up to a minute for a large PDF. A document is not answerable until indexing finishes; its status shows as processing until then.

Asking a question searches your documents two ways at once. One search matches on meaning, so "how long do I have to pay" finds a passage that says "invoices are payable within 30 days" despite sharing almost no words. The other matches on exact wording, which is what finds an error code or a product name that a meaning-based search would blur into something similar. The two result lists are then combined.

Every answer cites the passages it used. A citation is not decoration: the answer is generated only from those passages, and if they do not contain enough to answer, the system says so rather than guessing. An answer with no citations is a refusal, and that is working correctly.

Uploading a new version of a document replaces the old one for search purposes. Previous versions are retained but are never returned in answers, so an outdated policy cannot resurface once it has been superseded.

Conversations remember what was said earlier, so follow-up questions can use pronouns. "What about annual plans?" after a question about payment terms is understood as being about payment terms.
