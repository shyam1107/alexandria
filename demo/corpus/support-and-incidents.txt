Support and Incident Response

Support is available Monday to Friday, 08:00 to 18:00 UK time, excluding English bank holidays. Enterprise accounts additionally have weekend cover for critical issues only.

Tickets are triaged into four severities. Critical means the product is unavailable or data is at risk; we respond within one working hour and work continuously until there is a workaround. High means a major feature is unusable with no workaround; we respond within four working hours. Normal means degraded or inconvenient behaviour with a workaround; we respond within one working day. Low covers questions and feature requests; we respond within three working days.

Response time is time to first human reply, not time to resolution. We do not publish resolution targets, because a resolution time we could guarantee would be a resolution time we had padded.

Error code E429 means you have exceeded the request rate limit for your plan. It is returned with a Retry-After header indicating how long to wait. E429 is not an outage and does not count against uptime. If you see E429 during normal use rather than during a bulk import, contact support — it usually means an integration is retrying without backoff.

Error code E503 means the answering service is temporarily unavailable, usually because an upstream model provider is degraded. The system fails over to a secondary provider automatically; a sustained E503 means both providers are unavailable and will appear on the status page.

Planned maintenance is announced at least five working days in advance and scheduled between 22:00 and 02:00 UK time. Maintenance windows do not count against the uptime commitment. Emergency maintenance, which is rare, is announced as it happens.

The uptime commitment is 99.9% measured monthly, excluding planned maintenance. If we miss it, service credits are applied automatically to the next invoice — you do not need to claim them. Credits are capped at one month's fee.

To escalate a ticket, reply to it asking for escalation and state the business impact. Escalation is a routing decision, not a severity change; a Normal ticket that is escalated is still a Normal ticket, but a named engineer owns it.
